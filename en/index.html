How I think

The concepts in my everyday work

I use them every day, on every project, out of habit. Choose a concept to see a picture and an example from my work.

Discoverythe right problem before building

The Double Diamond

First I widen and narrow the problem, only then the solution.

Gobe: Facebook groups and toxicology data (discover), one user and the promise “teach people to look” (define), mock-ups (develop), closed test and launch (deliver).

Halcom: the portfolio overview was “discover and define” across all banks.

Source: Design Council ↗

Discovery principles

Most ideas don't work. I test them quickly and cheaply, with real people.

Gobe: the “snap and identify” idea didn't survive scrutiny. Testers reported a missing mushroom 33 times, so the app grew from 25 to 64 species before launch.

Source: Marty Cagan, SVPG ↗

The four big risks

Before building, I check value, usability, feasibility and viability.

Gobe: “don't know” from 60% to 8% (usability), everything works offline (feasibility), free because the sources allow only non-commercial use (viability).

Ceneje.si: 9 → 5 ad placements with the same revenue.

Source: Marty Cagan, SVPG ↗

The user and the friction

Who will use it, what they need, and where they get stuck.

Gobe: a beginner in the forest, one hand free and no signal. Hence big buttons, offline use, and colour only as a soft sign.

Source: Clayton Christensen, Jobs to Be Done ↗

Prioritieswhat first and what not

Impact and effort

First what brings the most for the least work.

Gobe: among my ideas (an app, a journal, a website, a widget), the mushroom app had the best ratio. The others are parked.

Source: impact–effort matrix ↗

Urgent and important

I separate what's important from what's urgent.

  • Do now: an immediate update is planned for any wrong fact about a poisonous mushroom.
  • Schedule: an expanded atlas in version 1.1.
  • Delegate: user accounts and saving known mushrooms.
  • Drop: ads in the first version.

Source: Eisenhower matrix ↗

Mindsetconfidence comes from understanding

Think again

I don't reject or back an idea until I see the whole picture. Confidence comes from experience and understanding.

Gobe: I changed decisions when the evidence came in. A demo with 8 mushrooms became a full launch; 25 species became 64.

Source: Adam Grant, Think Again (2021) ↗

How I work

From the whole picture to the order of work

I'm available for a single piece of work or a longer engagement. The approach stays the same; only the scope changes.

  1. The whole picture first

    I draw on one page what the problem is made of.

    You geta picture the whole team understands

  2. The foundation

    I find what has to hold for everything else to make sense. With Gobe, it was safety.

    You getclarity on what matters most and why

  3. The order of work

    I work out what depends on what and what can run in parallel.

    You getwhat comes first and what can wait

  4. Written down as I go

    I write down blind spots, decisions, reasons and trade-offs as they come up.

    You geta team that knows what it's building and why, even after I've left

What I help with

When to call me

  • You have an idea, but don't know if anyone will use it.

    From idea to first version

    I find out who it's for. Then mock-ups, a working first version of the main flow with analytics, and a test with real people.

  • The product exists, but people drop off somewhere.

    Product reviews

    I find where they get stuck, what to fix first and what to measure.

  • Development is under way, but nobody knows what matters most.

    Roadmap planning

    A plan: what comes first, what later and what not at all. With a reason for each decision.

  • You're planning a new feature.

    Feature reviews

    I check who it's for, what it must do and how you'll know it works.

  • You want to bring in AI or build something new, but don't know where to start.

    AI and digital innovation

    I find where AI saves time and where it doesn't. I help develop digital innovations and set up the digital and team processes around them.

  • You're building, but have no product manager.

    Fractional product manager

    I take on product management in your team: priorities, decisions and alignment.

AI in my work

I don't always deliver faster. I deliver better.

I've been using AI in my work for two years, and it's a big part of what I do as a product manager. It doesn't replace judgement.

  • Research and synthesis

    With ChatGPT and Claude I review the market, sources and edge cases. I check every source myself.

  • A knowledge base

    I keep notes, decisions and the project log in Obsidian. AI works from them, so it doesn't guess.

  • Prototypes with Claude Code

    The plan, the order and the rules are mine. AI writes the code; I review every step.

  • Judgement

    AI shows the options. Which one is right, I decide.
