How I work

First I find what everything rests on. Only then do I connect the rest.

I first see every project as a whole, from a distance. Then I go into the parts everything rests on, one by one, finding what wasn't visible from afar. In the end I know what depends on what and in which order we go.

  1. The whole picture first

    Before anything is built, I draw on one page what the problem is made of. No details yet, but every part.

    You getone view of the whole problem that the whole team understands

  2. What matters most

    I find what has to hold for everything else to make sense. With Gobe, it was safety.

    You getclarity on what matters most and why

  3. The order of work

    Only then do I connect it all: what depends on what, what can run in parallel and what can't.

    You getwhat comes first and what can wait

  4. Blind spots

    As the work goes on, I write down what couldn't be seen from afar, and for each finding what it changes.

    You getfewer surprises, and a decision for every risk

  5. Decisions on record

    In the end every decision, reason and trade-off is written down.

    You geta team that knows what it's building and why, even after I've left

Example · Gobe

An app that deliberately doesn't identify mushrooms

  1. I dropped the feature everyone expects.

    Identifying mushrooms from a photo would have brought more downloads, but it promises a certainty that doesn't exist. Instead, a quiz teaches what to look at.

  2. I measured before testing started.

    On the question about the volva, 12 out of 20 people answered “I don't know”. After I added photos to the help, 1 out of 12.

  3. From idea to Google Play in 23 days.

    During the test, testers reported 33 times that their mushroom was missing. Before launch, the app grew from 25 to 64 species.
